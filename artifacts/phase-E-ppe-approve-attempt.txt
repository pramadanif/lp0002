## Where it stopped, and why
  It did NOT complete. The proof was stopped deliberately, not because it errored:

    r0vm resident memory : ~4.4 GB
    system swap in use   : 7.8 GB of 9.2 GB
    free physical memory : ~100 MB

  The machine was thrashing. The recursive composition - env::verify over TWO chained
  programs inside LEZ's privacy-preserving circuit - needs succinct receipts, i.e. a
  lift+join for every segment of every inner program. That exceeds what this 8-core
  laptop has, so wall-clock time was dominated by swap, not by proving.

## What this does and does not establish
  ESTABLISHED: the composition is wired correctly end to end. The CLI selects the
    private path, resolves the dependency program, and hands a well-formed
    PrivacyPreservingCircuitInput to the prover. Nothing rejected the transaction.
  NOT ESTABLISHED: that the proof completes and the sequencer accepts it. No approval
    has been recorded on chain. Until one is, the composition remains DESIGNED AND
    WIRED, not DEMONSTRATED.

## What would finish it
  A machine with materially more RAM (this needs headroom well beyond 4.4 GB for the
  prover alone), or a GPU prover. This is a hardware constraint, not a design fault -
  but it is a real constraint on the claim 'proof generation runs client-side on a
  standard laptop' (P-F5) for the COMPOSED approval, as distinct from the standalone
  membership proof, which does complete in 53.26 s here.
